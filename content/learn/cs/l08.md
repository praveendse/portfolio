## 🟦 1. What is Information Retrieval (IR)?

### ✍️ Definition

**Information Retrieval (IR)** is the activity of obtaining **information resources relevant to an information need** from a **collection of information resources**.

### 🔎 Search can be based on:

- **Metadata**
- **Full-text indexing**
- Other content-based indexing

### Why do we need IR?

Because we have **too much information** → **information overload**.

> 🧠 **Remember:**  
> **Information Need → Search → Relevant Information**

---

# 🟨 2. Why Information Retrieval?

## 🚨 Information Overload

> **Information overload** = difficulty understanding an issue and making decisions because there is **too much information**.

As the amount of information grows, manually finding useful information becomes difficult.

### 🌐 Internet growth

The lecture uses the growth of the Internet as an example of the rapidly increasing amount of available information.

### 🧠 Main reason for IR

**More information → harder to find what matters → need IR.**

### Unstructured data

The idea was popularized by **Vannevar Bush's 1945 article _“As We May Think”_**.

### 💭 Memex

Bush described a **memex** as a device where an individual could store:

- Records

and consult them with **speed and flexibility**.

# 🟪 9. Core Concepts in IR

There are **three major concepts** to remember:

```text
        INFORMATION RETRIEVAL
                 │
       ┌─────────┼─────────┐
       ↓         ↓         ↓
     Query    Document   Retrieval
   Representation Representation  Model
```

---

## 9.1 🔎 Query Representation

A query represents the user's information need.

### Two important gaps

#### Lexical Gap

Different words can express related information.

Example from the lecture:

```text
say ↔ said
```

The words differ lexically even though they are closely related.

#### Semantic Gap

Different terms can refer to similar concepts.

Example from the lecture:

```text
ranking model ↔ retrieval method
```

> 🧠 **Remember:**  
> **Lexical = words**  
> **Semantic = meaning**

---

## 9.2 📄 Document Representation

Documents need to be represented in a form that allows efficient retrieval.

### Important idea

A **special data structure** is used for efficient access.

Document representation also has to deal with:

- Lexical gap
- Semantic gap

---

## 9.3 🎯 Retrieval Model

A retrieval model contains algorithms that find the **most relevant documents** for a given information need.

### 🧠 One-line definition

> **Retrieval model = algorithm for finding relevant documents.**

# 🆚 13. Information Retrieval vs Database Systems

| Information Retrieval | Database Systems |
|---|---|
| **Unstructured data** | **Structured data** |
| Object semantics are **subjective** | Object semantics are **well defined** |
| Simple keyword queries | Structured query languages |
| Example: keyword search | Example: SQL |
| **Relevance-driven retrieval** | **Exact retrieval** |
| Effectiveness is primary issue | Efficiency is emphasized |
| Efficiency is also important | Exact results are expected |

### 🧠 Fast comparison

```text
IR  → Unstructured → Keywords → Relevance
DB  → Structured   → SQL      → Exactness
```

# 🆚 15. Information Retrieval vs NLP

| Information Retrieval | Natural Language Processing |
|---|---|
| Computational approaches | Cognitive, symbolic & computational approaches |
| Statistical / shallow understanding | Semantic / deep understanding |
| Handles large-scale problems | Often handles smaller-scale problems |

### 🧠 Easy memory

```text
IR  → large scale + statistical/shallow
NLP → deeper semantic understanding
```

---

# 🔄 16. IR and NLP Are Getting Closer

## IR → NLP

- Larger data collections

## NLP → IR

NLP contributes:

- Deep analysis of text documents
- Deep analysis of queries
- Information extraction for structured IR tasks

### 🧠 Big picture

> **IR gives NLP scale; NLP gives IR deeper text understanding.**

# ✍️ Final “Write This in Your Notebook” Summary

> **Information Retrieval (IR)** is about finding information resources that are **relevant to a user's information need** from a collection of resources.
>
> IR became important because of **information overload** and the increasing amount of **unstructured data** such as text, images, audio and video.
>
> The three core concepts are **query representation, document representation and retrieval models**. IR must handle both **lexical gaps** and **semantic gaps**.
>
> IR is used in **web search, recommendation, question answering, text mining, online advertising and enterprise search**.
>
> **IR vs DB:** IR mainly handles **unstructured data** and focuses on **relevance**, while DB systems handle **structured data** and focus on **exact retrieval**.
>
> **IR vs NLP:** IR emphasizes **large-scale statistical/shallow processing**, while NLP emphasizes **deeper semantic understanding**.
>
> IR is interdisciplinary and connects with **DBs, NLP, ML, HCI, statistics, data mining, optimization and computer systems**.
>
> 🧠 **The whole lecture in one sentence:**  
> **“IR helps us find the right information from a huge amount of mostly unstructured information.”**
